# Product Requirements Document  
## Peptide Research Wiki Platform

**Status:** Ready for implementation planning  
**Product type:** Scientific research encyclopedia / peptide knowledge platform  
**Primary goal:** Build the most useful, transparent, consumer-readable peptide research reference possible while preserving scientific nuance, provenance, regulatory context, and safety information.

---

# 1. PRODUCT VISION

Build a modern web platform that functions as a **mini-Wikipedia for peptides and peptide-related research compounds**.

The platform should allow a visitor to:

- search for a peptide by name or synonym
- browse peptides by category
- understand what a peptide is
- understand how it is thought to work
- understand what has actually been studied
- distinguish cell, animal, observational, and clinical evidence
- see human clinical trials when they exist
- understand important uncertainties
- see regulatory status
- understand safety concerns and evidence gaps
- follow citations to primary scientific sources
- discover related peptides and research areas

The product must avoid the low-quality pattern common to many peptide websites where speculative claims, animal studies, anecdotal claims, and established human evidence are presented as though they are equivalent.

The defining product principle is:

> Every scientific claim should make clear what kind of evidence supports it.

The platform will support two audience-specific frontend experiences while sharing one canonical scientific knowledge base.

# 3. CORE ARCHITECTURAL PRINCIPLE

There must be **one canonical scientific dataset**.

Do NOT create separate scientific articles for the two audiences.

Architecture:

```text
CANONICAL PEPTIDE KNOWLEDGE BASE
              ↓
       Content Rendering Layer
          ↙          ↘
Women's Experience   50+ Experience
```

The following information must remain identical between audience experiences:

- scientific facts
- references
- evidence classifications
- regulatory status
- safety information
- uncertainties
- clinical trials
- data provenance

Only presentation may differ:

- layout
- typography
- homepage emphasis
- imagery
- copy framing
- information density
- discovery flows
- explanation depth

# 4. PRODUCT SCOPE

The MVP consists of six major systems:

1. Peptide Knowledge Database
2. Research Acquisition Pipeline
3. Evidence Classification System
4. Public Peptide Wiki
5. Research Admin / Review Interface
6. Two Audience Presentation Layers

# 5. NON-GOALS FOR MVP

Do not initially build:

- medical diagnosis
- personalized treatment recommendations
- dosage calculators
- cycle builders
- injection instructions
- individualized health recommendations
- AI doctor/chatbot
- automatic recommendations based on medical conditions
- user-generated medical claims
- forums
- social feeds
- subscription communities
- personalized health profiles

Ecommerce is not required for the initial MVP.

The architecture may support later linking to a separate commerce/catalog system, but scientific editorial content should remain logically separate from commercial product representations.

Do not build purchasing workflows for products whose sale would require legal or regulatory review unless separately authorized.

# 6. SOURCE HIERARCHY

Scientific information must originate from reputable primary or authoritative sources rather than other peptide-store marketing websites.

Preferred sources include:

## Tier 1: Government / authoritative databases

- FDA
- NIH
- PubMed
- ClinicalTrials.gov
- PubChem

## Tier 2: Specialist scientific databases

- IUPHAR/BPS Guide to Pharmacology
- RCSB Protein Data Bank
- UniProt where appropriate
- NCBI resources
- other high-quality curated biomedical databases

## Tier 3: Scientific literature

- systematic reviews
- meta-analyses
- randomized controlled trials
- clinical studies
- observational studies
- animal studies
- in-vitro studies

## Tier 4: Secondary explanation sources

Used only for orientation or discovery.

They must not be the sole basis for substantive scientific claims.

Examples:

- university research pages
- academic medical centers
- reputable scientific reviews
- regulatory summaries

Commercial peptide websites may be examined for:

- UX
- taxonomy
- page structure
- terminology
- catalog discovery
- disclaimer approaches

They must NOT be treated as authoritative scientific sources.

# 7. RESEARCH ACQUISITION SYSTEM

Create a repeatable pipeline that can build and update peptide records.

The pipeline should accept:

```text
canonical peptide name
optional aliases
optional seed identifiers
```

and retrieve or help researchers retrieve relevant information.

Expected source queries include:

```text
"<peptide>"
"<peptide>" human
"<peptide>" clinical trial
"<peptide>" randomized
"<peptide>" pharmacokinetics
"<peptide>" mechanism
"<peptide>" safety
"<peptide>" toxicity
"<peptide>" animal
"<peptide>" review
```

The system should support API retrieval where practical and browser/computer-use research where APIs are unavailable.

---

# 8. HUMAN-IN-THE-LOOP REQUIREMENT

The system must NOT automatically publish AI-generated scientific pages.

Required workflow:

```text
SOURCE DISCOVERY
      ↓
SOURCE INGESTION
      ↓
FACT EXTRACTION
      ↓
AI SYNTHESIS
      ↓
DRAFT
      ↓
REVIEW
      ↓
APPROVED
      ↓
PUBLISHED
```

Every peptide must have a status:

```text
unresearched
researching
draft
needs_review
approved
published
archived
```

AI may assist research and synthesis.

AI must not invent missing facts.

If evidence is absent or ambiguous, the system should explicitly store:

```text
unknown
insufficient evidence
conflicting evidence
not established
```

rather than infer a conclusion.

# 10. EVIDENCE MODEL

Every peptide should receive separate grades.

Do NOT assign one simplistic overall score.

At minimum display:

```text
Preclinical evidence
Human evidence
Clinical evidence
Safety evidence
Regulatory maturity
```

Suggested grades:

```text
Strong
Moderate
Limited
Very Limited
None Identified
Conflicting
```

An additional visual maturity scale may be:

```text
Level 0 — Mechanistic / theoretical only
Level 1 — In-vitro evidence
Level 2 — Animal evidence
Level 3 — Early human evidence
Level 4 — Controlled human evidence
Level 5 — Established clinical evidence
```

The system should never automatically imply:

```text
Level 2 → Level 4
```

Animal efficacy must remain clearly distinguishable from human evidence.

# 11. PEPTIDE PAGE INFORMATION ARCHITECTURE

Every peptide page should follow a standardized structure.

---

## Header

Display:

- peptide name
- aliases
- peptide class
- evidence badge
- regulatory badge
- last reviewed date

Include concise:

> What is this?

summary.

---

## Evidence Snapshot

Immediately visible.

Example:

```text
Preclinical Evidence      Moderate
Human Evidence            Very Limited
Clinical Trials           Limited
Long-Term Safety          Unknown
FDA Approved              No
```

Clicking a grade should explain how the grade was determined.

---

## Section 1 — Overview

Answer:

- What is it?
- Where did it come from?
- Is it naturally occurring or synthetic?
- Why are scientists studying it?

---

## Section 2 — How It Works

Describe:

- molecular targets
- pathways
- proposed mechanisms

Clearly label:

```text
Established mechanism
Supported hypothesis
Proposed mechanism
Unknown
```

Provide plain-English explanations alongside technical descriptions.

---

## Section 3 — Research Areas

Show areas scientists have investigated.

Example:

```text
Metabolic signaling
Tissue repair
Neurological function
Inflammation
Mitochondrial function
```

Never convert:

> “studied in connection with”

into:

> “improves”

unless appropriate evidence exists.

---

## Section 4 — Human Research

Prioritize this section.

Display:

- number of known human studies
- population
- study type
- sample size
- findings
- limitations

---

## Section 5 — Preclinical Research

```text

```

## Section 6 — Clinical Trials

Fields:

- trial name
- registry ID
- phase

- enrollment

- outcomes
- result availability

# 43. MVP ACCEPTANCE CRITERIA

The MVP is complete when:

1. At least 20 peptide records exist.

2. At least 5 are deeply researched reference-quality pages.

3. Every published substantive scientific claim has at least one source.

4. Evidence is visibly separated between:
   - cell
   - animal
   - human
   - clinical

5. Regulatory status appears on every peptide.

6. Unknown safety information is explicitly represented as unknown.

7. Users can search by peptide name and aliases.

8. Users can browse by research area.

9. Users can browse by evidence level.

10. Users can compare at least two peptides.

11. Clinical trials are linked when available.

12. Every peptide has a references section.

13. Researchers can draft and review pages.

14. AI-generated drafts cannot bypass editorial approval.

15. Both audience experiences render from identical scientific records.

16. Mobile and desktop layouts are complete.

17. Accessibility requirements are substantially met.

18. No medical recommendation or dosing system exists.

19. Provenance is retained for every important scientific claim.

20. The implementation has documentation sufficient for another engineer to continue development.

# 44. SUCCESS METRIC

The primary success test is:

> Can a curious non-specialist arrive knowing only the name of a peptide and leave understanding what it is, why researchers care about it, how much evidence actually exists, what remains unknown, and where to read the original science?

Secondary test:

> Can someone who does not know a peptide name browse the research landscape and learn without being pushed toward unsupported medical conclusions?

---

# 45. IMPLEMENTATION PRINCIPLES

Throughout implementation:

DO:

- prefer authoritative sources
- retain provenance
- distinguish evidence levels
- surface uncertainty
- explain terminology
- write original summaries
- cite aggressively
- design for comprehension
- separate research from marketing

DO NOT:

- scrape another seller and paraphrase its pages
- fabricate citations
- infer missing science
- convert animal evidence into human claims
- treat an AI summary as a source
- hide uncertain evidence
- generate medical recommendations
- build around SEO spam
- imply regulatory approval where none exists
